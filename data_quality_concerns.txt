╔══════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════╗
║  POSSIBLE EXPLANATIONS                                                        ║
╚══════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════╝

EXPLANATION 1: Multi-isoform issue
──────────────────────────────────
- "utr_len" = shortest isoform's 3'UTR
- "hits" = summed across ALL isoforms
- "hits_100bp" = hits in 100bp region of a SPECIFIC isoform

This would explain why a gene with utr_len=36bp has hits=515 but hits_100bp=0:
  - The 36bp UTR is from isoform A (minimal)
  - The 515 hits are from isoforms B, C, D which have longer UTRs
  - The 100bp window is evaluated on yet another reference

EXPLANATION 2: Hits are NOT in the UTR
─────────────────────────────────────
- "hits" might include the entire gene body (introns, 5'UTR, flanking regions)
- "utr_len" is still the 3'UTR length
- "hits_100bp" specifically means within 100bp of the stop codon

EXPLANATION 3: Counting methodology
──────────────────────────────────
- "hits" might be k-mer matches, not discrete TE insertions
- Each TE insertion could generate many "hits" due to overlapping seeds
- The 100bp window has a different counting method

EXPLANATION 4: Data generation artifact
───────────────────────────────────────
- Different annotation sources were mixed
- Some values are placeholders or estimates
- The pipeline had bugs for edge cases

╔══════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════╗
║  WHAT WE CAN'T CONCLUDE WITHOUT MORE INFO                                     ║
╚══════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════╝

1. We CANNOT confidently say "high-TE genes have shorter UTRs" because
   we don't know if utr_len is comparable between high and low TE groups

2. We CANNOT interpret "hits" as actual TE insertions without knowing
   the counting methodology

3. We CANNOT use "te_density" for meaningful comparisons because the
   formula appears to use potentially incompatible numerator/denominator

║  QUESTIONS TO RESOLVE                                                         ║
╚══════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════╝

1. How was this dataset generated?
   - What tool (BLAST? RepeatMasker? HMMER? Custom pipeline?)
   - What TE database (FlyBase TEs? Repbase? Custom?)
   - What was the query sequence (3'UTR only? whole transcript? whole gene?)

2. What does "utr_len" represent?
   - Shortest isoform? Longest? Canonical?
   - Is it from the same annotation version as the TE analysis?

3. What does "hits" count?
   - Individual TE insertions?
   - BLAST HSPs?
   - K-mer matches?
   - Why can a 36bp region have 515 "hits"?

4. What defines "hits_100bp"?
   - 100bp from what reference point?
   - Why can a gene with UTR < 100bp have hits_100bp = 0?

║  PATTERNS THAT MAY STILL BE MEANINGFUL                                        ║
╚══════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════╝

Even with these caveats, some patterns appear robust:

1. TE FAMILY SPECIFICITY - The FBti IDs differ dramatically between groups
   This suggests real biological signal regardless of counting issues

2. GENE IDENTITY - Master developmental regulators (Sxl, snail, etc.) are
   enriched in high-TE group. This is checkable in FlyBase directly.

3. EXTREME RANGE - The 4000x difference between high and low te_density
   groups (even if te_density formula is questionable) suggests a real
   difference in TE content.

4. BOTTOM GROUP CONSISTENCY - All 100 low-TE genes have hits_100bp=0,
   suggesting they may be genuinely TE-poor in proximal regulatory regions.
